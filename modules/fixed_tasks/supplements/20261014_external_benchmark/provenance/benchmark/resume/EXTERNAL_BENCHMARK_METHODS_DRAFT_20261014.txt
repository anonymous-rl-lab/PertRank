External-method comparison: Methods text for later manuscript integration
Working draft, 14 October 2026. This file does not modify manuscript v20.
Scientific wording updated from completed and verified reports; completed, paused and unevaluated scopes are identified separately. This is working text for later integration, not a modification of the frozen protocol or v20 manuscript.

Task definition and comparator roles
We evaluated perturbation nomination from 20 cells per target using the frozen candidate sets, query panels, controls, and deep reference for each benchmark task. Euclidean shrinkage was an internal ablation of PertRank and was not counted as an independent external method. External comparisons used the original FR-Perturb implementation and the official ashr posterior-mean estimator. These comparisons assess the specified nomination task rather than all tasks for which either external method was developed. They do not establish superiority over all existing perturbation-analysis methods.

FR-Perturb and the six-arm analysis
FR-Perturb was pinned to commit 24e653b6a5c050ab17f3600ebaf222bef32e2d25 of douglasyao/FR-Perturb. We retained its full-gene count input, TP10K and log1p preprocessing, centering by negative controls, sparse factorization, Lasso estimation, and native effect rescaling. The six arms were the raw effect estimate y, Euclidean-shrunk estimate w0*y, PertRank estimate wM*y, native FR estimate F, w0*F, and wM*F. Both scalar weights were calculated from the original input according to the frozen PertRank specification and were applied unchanged to the FR output. We did not recompute weights from FR-reconstructed effects. Differences between complete pipelines cannot be uniquely attributed to their low-rank component because their preprocessing, estimation, and rescaling also differ.

Development and parameter selection
The original FR development search used a single D3 Rest input containing 1,200 targets with 20 cells each and 2,000 negative-control cells. The search included ranks 5, 10, 20, 40, 50, 75, and 100 crossed with three Lasso penalties (1e-5, 1e-4, and 1e-3); sparse-PCA alpha, iteration count, and seed were fixed at 0.1, 50, and 20261012. Native D3 P@25 selected rank 100 and alpha 1e-4. The original selection and subsequent D4 protocol were retained during recovery. Fifty factorization iterations specify the computational procedure; they do not certify convergence of the learned dictionary. Solver and objective checks are reported separately. Historical PertRank and FR searches had similar configuration counts but different objectives and computational costs, so we do not describe them as strictly matched tuning budgets.

D3 manuscript-number reconciliation
The external-method development task used the original first 20-cell input group, corresponding to within-target positions 100:120. The manuscript's five-input summary averages five non-overlapping groups. The candidates, query panel, reference, controls, gene axes, and scalar-arm definitions agree. PertRank P@25 is 0.3051450054054106 for the first group and 0.31309885655412767 for the five-group mean. Every method in the external development comparison is evaluated on the first group; the manuscript mean is not substituted into that table. For the same reason, the appropriate raw comparator in this development table is 0.27714277780097235 rather than the five-input mean 0.2830717051374939.

ASH
ASH was run using ashr 2.2-63 with the official posterior-mean output. The fixed default arm used the uniform mixture prior and the fdr control method. Standard errors included both target-mean sampling variance and the actual negative-control mean variance on the same gene axis. Computational sharding preserved the specified per-target fit and full-gene inputs. All convergence flags, warnings, input and posterior identities, and task completion counts are retained. Recovery of the default D3 posterior exactly reproduced its historical SHA256 and score; this is a replay of a historically fixed selection, not a new registration preceding historical D4 access. The two alternative D3 prior configurations retain their historical records; their purely archival replay was paused. The D4 ASH comparisons were previously unfinished and were completed under the fixed selection. A generic recovery_replay tag in the implementation denotes recovery of the runtime and inputs, not proof that each D4 ASH analysis was previously complete.

D4 transfer and uncertainty
D4 Rest and Stim8hr pooled tasks contain 589 and 561 candidate targets, respectively, each evaluated over five non-overlapping 20-cell input groups. Each state retains its own 300-query panel and 30 query blocks. We summarize each method by averaging across the five fixed inputs and use paired query-block bootstrap samples to quantify query heterogeneity. These five inputs are not independent biological replicates: they share a donor, task definition, controls, reference, and geometry. The intervals are conditional on these fixed inputs and references and do not quantify donor-population uncertainty or all forms of cell-sampling uncertainty.

The two-state summary gives Rest and Stim8hr equal weight. It resamples the original query blocks separately within each state before averaging the state scores, rather than pairing queries by column position. Different query panels and shared biology mean that this is a summary of two fixed tasks from one donor, not two independent donor replications. P@25 is the primary nomination metric and P@10 is a secondary sensitivity metric. These overlap measures quantify agreement with the specified deep-reference rankings, not an independently validated biological true-positive rate. The deep reference is an operational benchmark rather than complete biological ground truth. No new independent RNA reference or Pearson-residual alternative-reference sensitivity was added in this continuation.

The original FR family comprises five contrasts within each task and K: FR minus raw, FR minus Euclidean, PertRank minus FR, weighted FR minus native FR, and PertRank-weighted FR minus Euclidean-weighted FR. The reported marginal 99% bootstrap intervals provide an approximate Bonferroni interpretation for that five-contrast family, conditional on the validity of each marginal interval. This label does not cover all ranks, states, depths, methods, K values, or later contrasts in the study. The additionally requested PertRank-minus-weighted-FR contrast and all ASH contrasts are descriptive. A difference with an interval spanning zero is not evidence of equivalence or noninferiority.

Independent-guide task
The already opened D4 Stim8hr independent-guide task specification uses 395 targets, 20 input cells from guide A, and a 200-cell reference from guide B. The complete historical FR reports located during this continuation concern a rank-20 pilot, not the locked rank-100 transfer. The remaining rank-100 independent-guide runs were paused, and historical FR summaries are not presented as a newly verified final rank-100 comparison. The previously unfinished ASH comparison was completed on this fixed independent-guide task. Its raw, Euclidean and PertRank arrays, per-query scores and joint bootstrap draws were verified bitwise against the frozen scalar evaluation, enabling a directly verified comparison of ASH and the scalar methods without rerunning FR solely for that cross-check. Its joint bootstrap resamples reference cells as well as query blocks. The joint interval is the appropriate uncertainty summary for claims involving independent-reference performance. The original reference-stability gate passed but its cross-guide signal and continuation gates failed; these quality decisions were retained without revision. The observed near-zero geometry increment limits the evidence for cross-guide benefit in this setting; it would not prove that geometry has no effect in all independent-guide settings. A significant result in a pooled task and a nonsignificant result here are not themselves a formal interaction test between task designs.

Post hoc rank extension
After the original results had been seen, we added rank 150, 200, and 300 on the identical D3 development input, preserving alpha 1e-4 and all other native settings. Before computing these scores, we specified that a strict increase over rank 100 would trigger a D4 pooled sensitivity analysis using the highest-scoring expanded rank, with the smaller rank winning numerical ties within 1e-12. All three ranks are reported. The selected expanded configuration is rank 300, whose native D3 P@25 is 0.27867193205519847. The ten additional D4 fits were completed using this fixed configuration, without replacing the original primary comparison or continuation rule. All expanded-rank task and equal-state summaries passed separate post-owner integrity checks and independent score audits; the common scalar arms and query resampling matched the original D4 evaluation. All expanded-rank contrasts are descriptive. These analyses use previously opened development and D4 tasks and are explicitly post hoc, not independent confirmation. Rank 300 remains a search boundary. The full expression-dimension configuration exceeds the available 8 GiB memory budget, and actual matrix rank was not established. Neither rank 300 nor the 1,200-target count is described as the full-rank optimum of native FR-Perturb.

Nested depth diagnostic
Before reading the added depth expression payload, we selected 250 D3 targets by a deterministic hash of their stable target identifiers. The diagnostic uses nested 20-, 50-, and 100-cell inputs at within-target positions 100:120, 100:150, and 100:200, with the same original 2,000 controls and the same 100-cell reference restricted to these targets. It is one nested input chain, not five independent 100-cell repetitions. Geometry parameters remain fixed; sample-size-dependent scalar weights and standard errors are recomputed. The 20-cell effects and weights must reproduce the corresponding original target subset within the frozen numerical tolerance.

FR depth curve A retains rank 100 and alpha 1e-4 at every depth. Secondary curve B holds the original effective penalty alpha*N/n=0.13 fixed, giving alpha=0.13*n/(250*n+2000). Both curves are reported without selecting the more favorable one after observation. ASH retains the fixed default prior. The candidate universe differs from the full 1,200-target development task, so absolute P@25 values are not directly compared between those tables. Within-depth method-minus-raw contrasts help interpret relative changes with input depth. Such changes do not by themselves establish implementation correctness or isolate unstable standard errors as their cause.

GPerturb resource gate
We pinned the native GPerturb implementation to commit 05d5a431413ff9dc443c82b994b571ecc563426d and used a CPU implementation gate before any performance comparison. The real-input gate used all 26,000 D3 development cells, 1,200 targets, 15,616 genes positive in the input, all 300 evaluation queries, and 26 technical covariates, with width 700. The disclosed full-gene/covariate adapter is one evaluable configuration rather than a unique requirement of GPerturb. Its native objective and validation logic were retained. The predeclared worker-RSS guard was 6.5 GiB with a 3,600-second gate budget. The worker reached 6.632 GiB RSS after 29.58 seconds and exited through the resource guard; no OS out-of-memory failure was recorded. The full resource gate was not completed. Because stage progress was not persisted before exit, the number of completed batches and the phase at termination are unknown. We do not report a performance score, convergence assessment, full-epoch runtime projection, or width-1000 result. This resource limitation is not counted as an algorithmic defeat. Alternative native-compatible gene panels or covariate adapters would require a separately locked evaluation and remain possible.

Recovery and evidence accounting
The original 21-configuration D3 FR results were restored without fitting the models again. The D3 default ASH posterior was replayed and exactly matched its historical posterior SHA256. D4 original-configuration FR tasks were completed and checked against available historical numerical summaries; those summaries do not establish that every historical full model output was saved or can be matched byte for byte. These restoration/check computations do not add independent validation evidence. New rank/depth diagnostics, the GPerturb resource gate, and previously unfinished ASH comparisons are identified separately. The historical completion status of the N-scaled FR sensitivity queue could not be established from available complete records. Its remaining runs, the remaining old independent-guide queue, and purely archival D3 ASH-prior replays were paused rather than presented as completed evidence.

Execution and scope
Completed tasks are identified by native fit completion, input and output integrity checks after the owning process has exited, and independent score verification. Storage interruptions and incomplete files are retained as execution failures and are not counted as method performance failures. Original scientific hashes and acceptance criteria are not relaxed during recovery. Only a completed, verified output is included in a performance table. New sensitivity protocols are local timestamped records; no claim of public OSF registration is made. D1/D2 Stim expression was not read in this run. Their historical access status has not been independently established, so they are not described here as certified untouched holdouts. GPerturb is reported according to its actual execution and convergence status and is not counted as a losing comparator if full evaluation is unavailable.
