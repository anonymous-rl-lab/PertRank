# Interpretation of the v20 S3 accounting

The original positive Stim metric increment is exactly recovered: 0.0151483 P@25, or 0.378708 reference-supported members per 25 nominations. The metric brings in 1.021590 supported members and loses 0.642882, averaged over the same queries, inputs and directions. This is an accounting of the already observed gain, not additional independent performance validation.

The actual net contributions are +0.235400 from low alignment, +0.311813 from middle alignment, −0.171497 from high alignment and +0.002993 from the 22 unclassified targets. Low and middle ranges are positive; the high-alignment range is negative. The data therefore directly identify a cost concentrated in the high-alignment group, while gains in the other two groups more than compensate. The middle group contributes the largest positive net; extrapolated tercile values derived from the regression slope alone were not accurate observed estimates. Mean signal-to-noise ranks differ across low/middle/high groups (0.304/0.577/0.621), so independent-rank approximations are inappropriate.

The interpretation concerns reallocation of a fixed list. The high-alignment group gains only 0.798 changed positions while losing 2.166. Its added positions nevertheless have greater reference support per slot than its removed positions (20.4% versus 15.4%). Thus a negative group net does not mean every substitution involving that group is inferior, nor does it validate removing a group or changing beta. No subgroup is reranked in this analysis.

The observed alignment coefficient is −0.000250355. The analytical uniform-reference coefficient is also negative (−0.000055669), confirming that algorithmic redistribution alone creates some negative alignment association. The observed-minus-neutral coefficient is −0.000194686, with conditional range [−0.000217972, −0.000177309]. The real reference therefore changes the association beyond the reference-agnostic expectation. Its stronger negative sign is not itself a success statistic: the gain/loss accounting shows both beneficial redistribution and lost supported high-alignment candidates. No artifact-removal, causal-mechanism or automatic deployment-threshold claim follows.

Zero-weight rescue is measurable but contributes little to this net gain. Euclidean-zero/PertRank-positive cases number 447 of 2,805 target-by-input combinations and contribute +0.008915 reference members. The both-positive/PertRank-higher category contributes +0.457752, whereas the both-positive/PertRank-lower category contributes −0.087618. These are descriptive classifications of candidates under simultaneous reranking, not isolated effects of increasing or decreasing a single weight. Even unchanged zero-weight candidates can change tie probabilities when competitors change.

The primary increment remains positive under every omitted original query block (0.014687–0.016002) and under the fixed 20/40-block resampling sensitivities (ranges [0.013175,0.018387] and [0.013174,0.017633]). The high-alignment net remains negative across the same checks. These observations address dominance by a single represented query module and dependence on the selected block count. They do not calibrate a donor-population confidence interval or replace the original 30-block decision.
